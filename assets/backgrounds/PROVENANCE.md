# Focus mode background photos: provenance

These photographs are in the public domain: works of US federal employees made as part of their
duties (17 U.S.C. §105). They are not covered by `assets/LICENSE`. Each file was scaled to 2048 px
on its long side and saved as JPEG (at most 400 KB) with macOS `sips`; the app softens and dims
it when shown. Chosen for no identifiable people, logos, insignia, text or legible ship names.

## Notes on license signals

- **NOAA Photo Library Flickr tag.** NOAA's Flickr account shows "CC BY 2.0" on every photo. The same account's profile says it "captures a selection of images from the public domain images residing in the NOAA Digital Collections". Wikimedia Commons tags these files `PD-USGov-NOAA`. A work made by a federal employee as part of their job has no copyright under 17 U.S.C. §105, so a CC licence can't add conditions to it. I only kept NOAA photos whose original caption names a NOAA employee, a NOAA Corps officer or NOAA ship personnel as the photographer.
- **NASA Johnson Flickr tag.** The Flickr copy of ISS040-E-33303 shows "CC BY-NC 2.0". The canonical NASA source is the JSC Gateway to Astronaut Photography, which lists the frame. Its FAQ asks only for a courtesy credit and says "It is unlawful to falsely claim copyright or other rights in NASA material". NASA crew photographs are US government works.
- Crediting is not legally required for any file. For the two ISS images, NASA asks for the courtesy line above.
- Considered and left out: a NOAA waterspout photo, because the photographer's federal employment
  could not be confirmed. Also photos by volunteers, a ship captain and other non-federal photographers,
  files marked CC BY on Commons, and frames with solar panels, people or lens flares.
